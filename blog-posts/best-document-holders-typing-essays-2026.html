How We Chose: Buying Guide

Start with where the holder will live. Freestanding weighted stands suit a fixed dorm desk because they hold single sheets and bound drafts steady at screen height, while folding metal stands pack flat for library sessions with textbooks. Clip-on holders that grip the monitor bezel use zero desk space and keep short notes at exact eye line, but they cannot carry heavy books and fit varies by monitor thickness.

Check holding power against your sources. Typing quotes from a few printed pages needs only a light copyholder with a line-guide ruler, whereas typing from a 500-page textbook or a thick draft needs a metal platen with page clips and angle locks. Look for 45 to 60 degrees of tilt beside the monitor so your eyes move sideways rather than down, and a non-slip base if you type hard enough to nudge light stands.

Weight and adjustability decide daily comfort. A one to three pound weighted base stays put during fast typing, nine or more angle steps let two flatmates share one stand, and fold-flat designs matter if you commute to the library. Budget plastic holders are fine for A4 printouts but flex under books, so spend up to the 3M or HUANUO metal options if textbooks are your main source. If you dictate essays with voice tools, the same stands hold prompt sheets beside the microphone, and left-handed writers should place the stand on the left to keep the mouse hand free.
