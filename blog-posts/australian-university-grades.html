    High Distinction, Distinction, Credit and Pass: Australian University Grades Explained

    Understand HD, Distinction, Credit and Pass, why the percentage bands are not identical at every Australian university, and how WAM and GPA relate to your results.

    Australian university gradesHigh DistinctionDistinctionCredit gradeinternational students

    Quick Answer

    How do Australian university grades work?

    Many Australian universities use grade categories such as High Distinction (HD), Distinction (D or DN), Credit (C or CR), Pass (P or PS) and Fail. The names are familiar across institutions, but the mark ranges are not universal. Always check your university’s official grading scheme.

    Common Australian University Grade Names

    International students often expect a single national grading table, but Australian universities set their own academic grading rules. Two universities can use the same label—such as High Distinction—while applying different percentage thresholds.

    GradeGeneral meaningTypical abbreviation
    High DistinctionOutstanding or exceptional performanceHD
    DistinctionSuperior or very strong performanceD / DN
    CreditGood, above-pass performanceC / CR
    PassSatisfactory achievement of course requirementsP / PS
    FailBelow the required standardN / F / FL

    Why High Distinction Does Not Have One National Cut-Off

    Consider two official examples. Monash University lists HD as 80–100, Distinction as 70–79, Credit as 60–69 and Pass as 50–59. UNSW Sydney lists HD as 85–100, Distinction as 75–84, Credit as 65–74 and Pass as 50–64.

    Both are valid Australian grading systems. This is why a statement such as “80 is always an HD in Australia” is incorrect. A mark must be interpreted using the rules of the university that awarded it.

    What Does High Distinction Mean?

    High Distinction is normally a top performance category. It indicates that the work has met a very high standard under the university’s marking criteria. However, the grade name alone does not tell you exactly why the work earned that result.

    Read the rubric and marker feedback. A strong paper may demonstrate depth of understanding, well-supported analysis, appropriate evidence, clear organisation and effective communication. Different disciplines may emphasise these qualities differently.

    What Is WAM?

    WAM stands for Weighted Average Mark. Rather than converting every result into a grade-point value, a WAM generally combines numerical marks while taking the credit value or weighting of each unit into account. A higher-credit unit may therefore contribute more to the final WAM than a smaller unit.

    The exact WAM formula, treatment of failed units, repeated courses, exchange study and other results depends on the institution. Use the official student handbook or results guidance when calculating or interpreting your WAM.

    What About GPA in Australia?

    Some Australian universities use GPA alongside or instead of WAM, but there is no single Australian GPA scale. You may encounter a 7-point system, a 4-point system or another institutional approach. This differs from the common US 4.0 GPA model.

    If an overseas university asks for your GPA, do not convert a WAM or Australian grade yourself unless the application specifically tells you how. Provide the original transcript and grading scale when requested. For comparison, see our guide to US college GPA for international students.

    Are Honours Grades the Same as Ordinary Unit Grades?

    Not necessarily. Honours programmes can use separate classifications such as First Class Honours, Second Class Honours Division A or B, or other structures. The thresholds and calculation rules may differ from the HD/Distinction/Credit/Pass table used for individual coursework units.

    Similarly, a postgraduate programme may have its own award rules. Always distinguish between the grade for one unit, your WAM or GPA, and the classification attached to the final degree.

    What International Students Should Check

    • The official grade legend on your transcript or university website.
    • Whether the institution uses WAM, GPA, or both.
    • The credit weighting of each unit.
    • Rules for repeated, failed or ungraded units.
    • Entry-equivalency requirements for postgraduate study abroad.
    • Whether an employer or university wants the original grade rather than a self-converted result.

    If you are comparing Australia with the UK, remember that a UK First or 2:1 is a final honours classification, not a simple label that can be matched mechanically to HD or Distinction. Our UK degree classifications guide explains the difference.

    Common Mistakes When Reading Australian Grades

    Do not assume that a mark range from one Australian university applies nationally. Do not treat WAM and GPA as interchangeable, and do not compare final honours classifications with individual unit grades without checking the rules. Most importantly, use the official grade legend attached to your institution and study period.

    Key Takeaways

    • HD, Distinction, Credit and Pass are common Australian grade labels.
    • The percentage ranges vary between universities.
    • Monash and UNSW provide clear examples of different valid thresholds.
    • WAM is a weighted average of marks, while GPA uses grade-point values.
    • Australian GPA scales are not universally the same.
    • Use official institutional rules for conversion, progression and award decisions.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    What does High Distinction mean in Australia?
    High Distinction, usually abbreviated HD, is a top grade category used by many Australian universities. The mark threshold varies: for example, Monash uses 80–100 while UNSW uses 85–100 in its standard grade scheme.
    Is 75 a Distinction or High Distinction in Australia?
    It depends on the university. At Monash, 75 falls in Distinction because HD begins at 80. At UNSW, 75 is also Distinction because HD begins at 85. Other institutions may use different ranges.
    What does Credit mean at an Australian university?
    Credit is a passing grade above Pass and below Distinction in many Australian systems. The exact mark range varies by institution; examples include 60–69 at Monash and 65–74 at UNSW.
    What is WAM in Australia?
    WAM means Weighted Average Mark. It combines numerical marks using course or unit weightings. The exact calculation rules can vary by university and programme.
    Is Australian GPA always out of 4.0?
    No. Australian universities may use different GPA scales, including 4-point or 7-point systems, and some focus more on WAM. Never assume one university’s GPA scale applies to another.
    Can I convert an Australian HD directly to a US A?
    Not reliably by yourself. Institutions may assess international grades using their own conversion policies, so official admissions or credential-evaluation guidance should be used when a formal equivalence is required.
    Is a C− a pass in Australia?
    A C− does not have one universal meaning across Australian universities. Do not confuse it with C or CR, which can mean Credit. Check the grading legend on your transcript and your institution’s official pass requirements.
    Is 65 a Credit at an Australian university?
    At UNSW, 65 falls within the Credit range of 65–74. At Monash, it also earns a Credit, within the 60–69 range. Other universities may use different thresholds, so check your institution’s official grading scale.
    Is a Credit a good grade at university?
    A Credit generally represents good performance above a Pass and below a Distinction. Whether it meets your goals depends on your course requirements and any entry thresholds for honours, postgraduate study or scholarships.
