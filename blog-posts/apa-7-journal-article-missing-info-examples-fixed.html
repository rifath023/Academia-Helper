APA 7th Journal Article Reference Examples: Missing DOI, No Author & More (Fixed)

The 4 APA 7 Manual Rules You Must Know First

All 15 fixes below follow the official Publication Manual of the American Psychological Association (7th ed.). Four rules solve 90% of problems:

  • 1. DOI always as https://doi.org/: Never write doi:10.xxxx or http://dx.doi.org. APA 7 requires the full hyperlink format: https://doi.org/10.1037/arc0000014. No full stop after the DOI.
  • 2. No author → move title to author position: Do not write “Anonymous” or “Unknown.” Move the article title before the date. In-text, use a shortened title in quotes: (“Title Words,” Year).
  • 3. No date → use (n.d.): If no year can be found even on the publisher page, use (n.d.) in both in-text and reference list. Do not guess the year.
  • 4. 20-author rule: List up to 20 authors in full. For 21+, list the first 19, insert an ellipsis (…), then add the final author. In-text for 3+ authors: First Surname et al. from the first citation.
Golden rule: cite the version you actually used, but prefer the publisher version with a DOI over an academia.edu citation, ResearchGate copy, or PDF from a university repository.

15 Copy-Paste APA 7 Journal Fixes: Wrong → Right

Each fix shows the mistake students make, the corrected reference-list entry, and the matching in-text citation. Replace the examples with your own details but keep punctuation, italics and order identical.

1
Missing DOI (print or older article)
Most common
❌ Wrong: Ahmed, S. (2008). Study habits and GPA. Journal of Education, 12(3), 45–59. Retrieved from EBSCOhost.
✅ Right (reference list): Ahmed, S. (2008). Study habits and GPA. Journal of Education, 12(3), 45–59.
In-text: (Ahmed, 2008) / Ahmed (2008) found …
Why: If Crossref shows no DOI, do not invent one and do not add a database name or proxy link. End after the page range. Only add a URL if the article is openly available on the journal site. Need a refresher on structure? See our APA 7th edition citation guide.
2
No author — the classic APA 7 no-author example
High-risk
❌ Wrong: Anonymous. (2021). Effects of sleep. Journal of Cognitive Research, 15(2), 45–59.
✅ Right (reference list): Effects of sleep on memory consolidation. (2021). Journal of Cognitive Research, 15(2), 45–59. https://doi.org/10.1037/cog0000123
In-text: (“Effects of Sleep,” 2021) — use first few words of title in quotes.
Why: This is the textbook APA 7 journal reference missing DOI no author example: when no person or organisation is credited, the title moves to the author position, before the date. Alphabetise by first significant word (“Effects”) in your list. Never use “Unknown” or “N.A.”
3
No date (n.d.)
Copy-paste
❌ Wrong: Patel, R. (2020?). Mindfulness in schools. Wellbeing Journal, 8(1), 10–22.
✅ Right (reference list): Patel, R. (n.d.). Mindfulness in schools. Wellbeing Journal, 8(1), 10–22. https://doi.org/10.1080/12345678
In-text: (Patel, n.d.) / Patel (n.d.) argues …
Why: If the publisher page, PDF header and Crossref show no year, use (n.d.) for “no date” in both places. Do not guess, write “forthcoming,” or leave brackets with a question mark.
4
Missing volume, issue or page numbers
Incomplete PDF
❌ Wrong: Lee, K. (2022). AI feedback. Computers & Education. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104512
✅ Right (reference list): Lee, K. (2022). AI feedback and student revision. Computers & Education, 185, 104512. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2022.104512
In-text: (Lee, 2022)
Why: Omit only what is truly missing. If there is no issue number, give Journal, Volume, pages. If there are no pages yet, give the article number (see Fix 10). Check the publisher page — PDFs downloaded from Academia.edu often strip volume info that the DOI page still has.
5
DOI formatted the old way
Easy marks
❌ Wrong: … Psychological Science, 33(4), 512–528. doi:10.1177/09567976211038222 / Retrieved from https://dx.doi.org/…
✅ Right (reference list): Garcia, M., & Thompson, L. (2022). Sleep and exam performance. Psychological Science, 33(4), 512–528. https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976211038222
In-text: (Garcia & Thompson, 2022)
Why: APA 7 requires DOIs as clickable https://doi.org/ links, no “doi:” prefix, no “Retrieved from,” and no full stop at the end. Generators reviewed in our best citation generators guide still export the old format — always fix it manually.
6
21 or more authors — the 20-author rule
Exam favourite
❌ Wrong: Listing all 27 authors, or cutting with “et al.” in the reference list.
✅ Right (reference list): Smith, A., Jones, B., … [first 19 authors] … , & Zhao, Z. (2023). Global learning outcomes. Nature Human Behaviour, 7(5), 701–715. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01555-0
Pattern: Author 1 … Author 19, . . . Last Author. (Year). …
In-text: (Smith et al., 2023) from the very first citation.
Why: For 1–20 authors, list everyone. For 21+, list the first 19, insert an ellipsis (…), then the final author. Omit the middle names entirely. “et al.” is only for in-text citations, never in the reference list.
7
Organisation / corporate author
WHO, APA, OECD
❌ Wrong: WHO. (2023). Mental health report. WHO Bulletin, 101(2), 88–97.
✅ Right (reference list): World Health Organization. (2023). Global mental health workforce shortages. Bulletin of the World Health Organization, 101(2), 88–97. https://doi.org/10.2471/BLT.22.28934
In-text: (World Health Organization [WHO], 2023), then (WHO, 2023).
Why: Spell out the full organisation name in the reference list, never just the acronym. If the organisation is both author and publisher (common for reports), you still follow the journal pattern when it is a journal article — author is the organisation, source is the journal.
8
Preprint (PsyArXiv, arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN)
Pre-publication
❌ Wrong: Citing a PsyArXiv paper as if it were already in a journal: Chen, L. (2024). Motivation. Journal of Psychology, 20(1), 1–15.
✅ Right (reference list): Chen, L., & Davies, P. (2024). Motivation interventions in first-year undergraduates [Preprint]. PsyArXiv. https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/abc12
In-text: (Chen & Davies, 2024)
Why: Unpublished preprints have no journal, volume or pages. Give author, year, title in italics? No — title in sentence case (not italicised differently), then [Preprint] in square brackets, repository name, and DOI or URL. If it later gets published, update to the journal version.
9
Advance online publication (no volume / issue yet)
Online first
❌ Wrong: Khan, A. (2024). Teacher burnout. Teaching & Teacher Education. https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx
✅ Right (reference list): Khan, A. (2024). Teacher burnout after remote schooling. Teaching and Teacher Education, 135, Article 104123. Advance online publication. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tate.2024.104123
In-text: (Khan, 2024)
Why: When the article is published online before assignment to a volume/issue, add the words Advance online publication before the DOI. Once volume and pages are assigned, remove that phrase and update the reference.
10
Article number / eLocator instead of pages
PLOS, Nature, Frontiers
❌ Wrong: Mensah, D. (2023). Open data. PLOS ONE, 18(6), 1–15. https://doi.org/…
✅ Right (reference list): Mensah, D., Okafor, J., & Liu, Y. (2023). Open data reuse in African universities. PLOS ONE, 18(6), Article e0286543. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0286543
In-text: (Mensah et al., 2023)
Why: Many online-only journals use article numbers, not page ranges. Write Article e0286543 in place of pages. Keep the volume and issue if given. Do not invent “pp. 1–15” from the PDF page count.
11
No DOI but freely available online (use URL)
Open access
❌ Wrong: Hassan, N. (2021). Reading strategies. ELT Journal. Retrieved from EBSCO / No link at all.
✅ Right (reference list): Hassan, N. (2021). Reading strategies of bilingual learners. ELT Journal, 75(3), 301–312. https://academic.oup.com/eltj/article/75/3/301/1234567
In-text: (Hassan, 2021)
Why: This is the other half of the complete APA 7 reference fix for missing DOIs: if there is genuinely no DOI but the publisher provides a stable URL, give that URL (no “Retrieved from”). Never give a library database URL — use the publisher or journal homepage link.
12
Academia.edu / ResearchGate copy
academia.edu citation
❌ Wrong: Brown, T. (2020). Assessment. Studies in Education, 10(2), 55–70. https://www.academia.edu/98765432/assessment_paper
✅ Right (reference list): Brown, T. (2020). Formative assessment and feedback literacy. Studies in Higher Education, 45(2), 55–70. https://doi.org/10.1080/03075079.2020.1716329
In-text: (Brown, 2020)
Why: An academia.edu citation should be your last resort. Search the title on Crossref or the journal site, find the DOI, and cite the publisher version. Only keep the Academia.edu URL if the paper exists nowhere else — then give that URL instead of a DOI, and cite exactly the version you read. Never list both DOI and Academia.edu link.
13
In-press / forthcoming article
Accepted, not yet published
❌ Wrong: Wilson, E. (2024). Peer tutoring. Learning & Instruction (in print soon).
✅ Right (reference list): Wilson, E. (in press). Peer tutoring and retention in STEM. Learning and Instruction. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2024.101999
In-text: (Wilson, in press)
Why: For an article accepted but not yet published, replace the year with (in press). Omit volume, issue and pages until assigned. Update to the year once published. Do not use “forthcoming” or “submitted” — if it is only submitted, cite it as an unpublished manuscript instead.
14
Non-English / translated title
International sources
❌ Wrong: Dubois, C. (2021). Les stratégies d’apprentissage. Revue Française, 30(1), 12–25.
✅ Right (reference list): Dubois, C. (2021). Les stratégies d’apprentissage [Learning strategies]. Revue Française de Pédagogie, 30(1), 12–25. https://doi.org/10.4000/rfp.10234
In-text: (Dubois, 2021)
Why: Keep the original title, then add the English translation in square brackets, not italics. Keep accents exactly as published. If you read the translated version, note that — otherwise cite the original and provide your own translation in text.
15
Journal title abbreviated or in wrong case
Formatting marks
❌ Wrong: Taylor, J. (2022). Feedback timing. J. of educ. psych., 114(2), 200–215.
✅ Right (reference list): Taylor, J. (2022). Feedback timing and motivation. Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(2), 200–215. https://doi.org/10.1037/edu0000701
In-text: (Taylor, 2022)
Why: Write the full journal name in Title Case and italics, plus the volume in italics: Journal of Educational Psychology, 114(2). Never abbreviate unless the journal itself uses an abbreviation. Check the journal homepage, not Google Scholar, for the official title.

How to Find a Missing DOI in 60 Seconds (Crossref Tips)

Before you declare a DOI “missing,” run this 4-step check. Most “missing DOI” cases are just DOIs students did not look for:

  1. Crossref.org search: Go to search.crossref.org, paste the exact article title in quotes, and copy the DOI link shown. This catches 80% of cases.
  2. Publisher page first page: Open the article on Taylor & Francis, Elsevier, Springer, Wiley or the journal site. The DOI is usually at the top under “Cite this article” or in the URL itself.
  3. Click the DOI to verify: Paste https://doi.org/10.xxxx/xxxxx into your browser. If it resolves to your article, it is correct. If it 404s, you copied it wrong.
  4. Only then declare “no DOI”: If Crossref, the publisher, and the PDF show nothing — typically pre-2000 print articles — follow Fix 1 and end after pages. Do not substitute an Academia.edu or ResearchGate link when a publisher page exists.

Your Complete APA 7 Reference Fix Checklist (Tick Before Submitting)

  • Every journal reference ends with either a https://doi.org/ link or a publisher URL — never both, never a database name.
  • No “doi:”, “dx.doi.org”, “Retrieved from”, or full stop after the DOI.
  • Authors use Surname, Initials with & before the last: Smith, J. A., & Lee, K.
  • No-author entries start with the title; no-date entries use (n.d.).
  • 21+ author papers use first 19 … last author; in-text uses et al.
  • Article titles in sentence case; journal titles + volume in italics, Title Case.
  • Academia.edu / ResearchGate links replaced with publisher DOI where possible.
  • Hanging indent (0.5 inch), double-spaced, alphabetical reference list.

Final Thoughts: Stop Losing Marks for Missing Info

You do not need to memorise the whole APA manual. Memorise the base formula, the four rules at the top (DOI as https://doi.org/, no author → title first, no date → n.d., 20-author rule), and the Crossref check for missing DOIs — and you can produce a complete APA 7 reference fix for any journal article in under two minutes.

If your list still feels risky — mixed Academia.edu links, preprints, in-press papers, or a supervisor who is strict about italics and punctuation — get a second pair of eyes before you submit. One wrong DOI format or misplaced title can cost you a grade band.
