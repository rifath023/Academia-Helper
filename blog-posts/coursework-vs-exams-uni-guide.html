Coursework vs Exams: Check How You Will Be Tested Before Choosing a Uni

The same degree can feel totally different depending on assessment. Learn how to read handbooks and weightings, survive AI-era vivas, and pick the test style that lets you shine.

coursework vs examshow university assessment worksuni exams vs courseworkassessment style 2026

Understanding coursework vs exams before you apply can be the difference between a degree you enjoy and three years of dread — and in 2026 the assessment landscape is shifting fast. Student Voice research finds 46% of students want high-stakes exams rethought, while LSE's 2024–25 survey found 57% of students said the assessment format had put them off taking a course at all. Tellingly, those students preferred open-book and take-home exams (33%) with coursework second — and cited time pressure (72%) and limited resources (62%) as their biggest exam anxieties. This guide explains how university assessment works, shows you how to decode handbooks and weightings, quizzes you on which style suits you, and briefs you on AI-era vivas.

The Main Assessment Types, Explained

Every course mixes the formats below in different ratios. Two universities can teach near-identical content yet test it in opposite ways — which is exactly why you must check before choosing.

Watch the direction of travel: a Times Higher Education analysis from June 2025 warns against over-assessment — piling on endless low-stakes tasks can exhaust students just as badly as one terrifying final. The sweet spot is continuous low-stakes assessment with feedback, not a dozen deadlines a week. When comparing courses, count the number of assessments per term, not just their type.

How to Check the Handbook and Weighting (5-Minute Method)

You do not need insider access — universities publish this. For each shortlisted course, do the following:

  1. Open the course page and find the "modules", "course structure" or "assessment" tab.
  2. Open 3–4 module entries in the module catalogue or course management system — institutions such as Ulster and Edinburgh publish per-module pages showing exactly how each module is assessed.
  3. Read the weighting line: e.g. "Exam 60%, Coursework 40%". Note which modules are 100% exam — especially in first year when you are still adjusting.
  4. Check the exam format: closed-book, open-book, take-home or online? LSE 2024–25 data shows students strongly prefer open-book and take-home (33%) — if a course is all closed-book halls exams, know that going in.
  5. Check resits and condonement: can you resit a failed exam in August? Does passing coursework compensate a failed exam? Rules vary by university and matter enormously.
  6. Email admissions one question if anything is unclear: "What proportion of Year 1 assessment in this course is closed-book exam?" They answer this daily.
If two offers look equal on prestige, pick the one whose assessment mix matches your strengths — it can be worth a full degree classification over three years.

Which Suits You? The 60-Second Quiz

Answer honestly — there are no wrong answers, only expensive mismatches:

  • 1. Your ideal deadline is… (a) one big date I sprint towards, or (b) weekly milestones with feedback? Sprinters suit exams; steady builders suit coursework and patchwork.
  • 2. Under time pressure you… (a) focus sharply, or (b) go blank and panic? Only (a) should choose exam-heavy courses — 72% of LSE respondents named time pressure their top exam fear.
  • 3. Your best school marks came from… (a) timed tests, or (b) projects and essays? Past performance predicts future performance.
  • 4. Speaking up in class feels… (a) natural, or (b) awful? If (b), be cautious of presentation- and viva-heavy courses — or plan to train the skill deliberately.
  • 5. You prefer feedback… (a) once, as a final grade, or (b) often, while you can still improve? Coursework and problem sets give (b); finals give (a).
  • Mostly (a)? Lean exam-heavy. Mostly (b)? Lean coursework and continuous assessment. Split? Choose mixed or patchwork assessment — the safety net most students actually want.

One encouraging finding: when Carnegie Mellon researchers (via Inside Higher Ed) let students choose, 90% picked harder problem sets — and invested 3.6 times more study time. Given ownership, students do not dodge rigour; they embrace the format they trust. Pick the format you trust, and you will work harder without being forced.

AI-Era Assessment: Vivas, Presentations and Quizzes

Generative AI has changed the rules. Guardian reporting by Tess Reidy from September 2025 describes universities rethinking assessment: more presentations, vivas and in-class quizzes, plus renewed emphasis on dissertations students must be able to discuss fluently. The logic is simple — anyone can paste an essay prompt into a chatbot, but nobody can fake a 15-minute spoken defence of methods, choices and conclusions.

What this means for you:

  • Expect at least one viva or presentation per year, even on essay-based courses. Treat every submitted essay as something you may have to defend aloud.
  • Keep your process evidence: drafts, notes, data, reading logs. Some departments now ask for them alongside final submissions.
  • Dissertations matter more, not less: a supervised, discussed project is the hardest work to fake — and the strongest signal to employers.
  • Learn to talk about your work: after finishing any assignment, practise a two-minute spoken summary: question, method, key finding, one limitation. That habit prepares you for vivas automatically.

Tips for Each Format: Exams, Coursework and Everything Between

Acing exams (closed- and open-book)

  • Past papers are the syllabus: do three timed papers per module; examiners repeat structures yearly.
  • For open-book, tab ruthlessly: the LSE preference (33%) exists because application beats memorisation — but only if you can find anything in 60 seconds. Index your notes.
  • Attack time pressure directly: allocate minutes per mark before you enter, and move on when the budget is spent — the 72% who fear time pressure usually lose marks to one stuck question.
  • Sleep beats cramming: memory consolidates overnight; an extra two hours of sleep outperforms an extra two hours of notes.

Acing coursework and dissertations

  • Start ugly and early: a rough 500 words in week one beats a perfect plan in week four. Drafting time is where coursework marks come from — a literature review synthesis (Taylor & Francis) finds coursework yields higher marks than exams alone.
  • Use feedback loops: office hours, writing centres and draft reviews exist precisely because coursework rewards iteration.
  • Reference as you go: dropped citations and rushed bibliographies are the commonest coursework mark-losers.
  • A note on grade patterns: researcher Stankov (2023) links coursework-heavy systems to grade inflation debates — so departments moderate hard. Aim clearly above the pass band, not near it.

Acing presentations, vivas and quizzes

  • Rehearse aloud three times — silently reading slides does not count. Record one run on your phone.
  • Prepare for five hard questions on your weakest point; examiners always ask there.
  • For weekly quizzes and problem sets: never skip one — small marks compound, and they double as spaced revision for finals.
Integrity note: with AI-detection and vivas rising, submit only work you fully understand and can explain. If you use AI tools for brainstorming, check your university's policy first — and never submit text you could not defend in person.

Final Thoughts

The coursework vs exams choice is really a choice about where your marks come from: slow, resourced, improvable work — or fast, pressured, one-shot performance. With 46% of students wanting high-stakes exams rethought (Student Voice), 57% avoiding courses over format (LSE 2024–25), and AI pushing every department towards vivas and dissertations (Guardian, September 2025), the universities are already adapting. Do your five-minute handbook check on every shortlisted course, take the quiz above honestly, and choose the arena where you play best.
