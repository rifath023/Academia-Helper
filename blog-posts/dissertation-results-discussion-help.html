    Dissertation Results and Discussion Help: How to Interpret Findings and Build a Strong Chapter

    A practical guide to moving from raw dissertation findings to clear interpretation, comparison with literature and defensible conclusions.

    dissertation discussionresults chapterfindingsinterpretationresearch writing

    Quick Answer

    How do you build a strong dissertation results and discussion chapter?

    Present the findings clearly, then explain what they mean in relation to the research questions. Compare them with previous studies, discuss plausible explanations for agreement or disagreement, acknowledge limitations, avoid overstating causation and show how the evidence supports the final conclusion.

    Know Whether Results and Discussion Are Separate

    Some dissertations use separate results and discussion chapters; others combine them. Follow your programme or supervisor guidance. In either structure, keep a clear distinction between what the data show and how you interpret those findings.

    A combined chapter can work well when each finding is immediately discussed, while separate chapters may suit projects with many tables or complex analysis.

    Present Results Selectively

    Do not paste every software table or every interview quote. Select evidence that answers the research questions. Use clean tables, figures or themes and guide the reader through the important pattern.

    Move From Result to Interpretation

    After presenting a finding, explain what it means. For example, a positive regression coefficient needs contextual interpretation, not just a p-value. A qualitative theme needs explanation of how the participant evidence supports it and what the pattern suggests.

    Ask, “So what does this finding tell us about the research question?”

    Compare Findings With Previous Research

    The discussion should reconnect with the literature review. Identify where your findings agree, differ or add nuance. Do not simply repeat citations from the literature chapter; use them to interpret the new evidence.

    When studies disagree, consider differences in sample, context, measurement or method before declaring one result wrong.

    Handle Unexpected or Non-Significant Findings

    Unexpected findings are not failures. Check the analysis carefully, then discuss plausible explanations supported by evidence. A non-significant quantitative result may still inform the research question, and a surprising qualitative theme may reveal something the initial framework did not anticipate.

    Avoid changing the story only to make the dissertation match the original hypothesis.

    Discuss Limitations Without Undermining the Whole Study

    A limitation should explain the boundary of the evidence. Examples include sample size, response bias, measurement limitations, restricted context or missing data. Explain how the limitation affects interpretation and what future work could address it.

    Do not write a long list of generic limitations that have no connection to the actual design.

    A Practical Discussion Paragraph Structure

    StepPurpose
    FindingState the relevant result briefly
    MeaningExplain what it suggests
    ComparisonConnect to previous research
    ExplanationDiscuss why agreement or difference may exist
    ImplicationExplain what follows for the question or practice
    LimitationQualify the conclusion where needed

    For Qualitative Dissertations: Use Quotes Selectively

    Participant quotes should illustrate an analytical point rather than replace analysis. Introduce the theme, use a concise quotation, interpret it and show how it connects to the wider pattern.

    The interview quote presentation guide can help you balance evidence and interpretation.

    Make the Discussion Lead to the Conclusion

    By the end of the discussion, the reader should understand what the study found, how confident you are in those findings and what they mean. The conclusion should then answer the research questions directly rather than introduce a new argument.

    Interpret Honestly and Transparently

    Do not overstate causation, hide inconvenient findings or rewrite the interpretation only to match expectations. Strong academic discussion shows judgement, including uncertainty and limitations.

    Academic integrity note: Universities set their own rules for tutoring, editing, collaboration, AI use and outside academic assistance. Use professional support for permitted learning help such as planning, feedback, proofreading or technical explanation, and check your institution's policy before submission. The final research decisions and submitted work should remain yours.

    Map Findings Back to the Research Questions

    Create a simple table listing each research question, the evidence that answers it and the discussion point that follows. This reveals missing interpretation and prevents the discussion from spending too much space on interesting but secondary findings.

    Use the map again when writing the conclusion so each question receives a direct, evidence-based answer.

    Avoid Repeating the Results Without Adding Meaning

    A weak discussion often repeats the number, table or theme and then moves on. After every major result, add interpretation: why might this pattern exist, how does it compare with previous research, what does it imply for the research question and how strongly can you make that claim?

    For quantitative findings, do not turn the discussion into another list of coefficients. Use the statistics as evidence for the argument. For qualitative findings, do not let participant quotations replace your analytical voice. The reader should always know what you think the evidence shows and how that judgement connects to the dissertation purpose. This shift from repetition to interpretation is one of the clearest differences between a results section and a strong discussion chapter.

    Key Takeaways

    • Separate evidence from interpretation.
    • Report only findings that answer the research questions.
    • Reconnect the discussion to previous research.
    • Treat unexpected findings as evidence to understand.
    • Use limitations to define boundaries, not destroy the study.
    • Make the discussion lead directly to the conclusion.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Should results and discussion be separate chapters?
    It depends on your programme and research design. Follow institutional and supervisor guidance.
    How much literature should I use in the discussion?
    Use enough relevant literature to interpret key findings and compare agreement, disagreement and implications.
    What if my hypothesis is not supported?
    Report and interpret the result honestly. A non-supported hypothesis can still contribute useful evidence.
    Should I include every SPSS table?
    No. Include the output needed to answer the research questions and present it in a clear format.
    How many interview quotes should I use?
    Use enough to support the analytical point without allowing quotations to replace interpretation.
    Can someone help me interpret dissertation findings?
    Yes through permitted academic or statistical guidance, provided you understand and remain responsible for the final interpretation.
