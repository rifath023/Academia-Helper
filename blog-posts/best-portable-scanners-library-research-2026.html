How We Chose: Buying Guide

Choose overhead for books and sheetfed for loose pages. Overhead A3 scanners photograph bound volumes without flattening spines and auto-correct page curl, which archives prefer for rare texts, but they need laptop plus stand space. Sheetfed portables pull single pages at 5 to 16 pages per minute for journal stacks and handouts, though forcing bound books through them damages spines. Dissertation students with both sources often pair one overhead at home with phone scanning in stacks.

Resolution and software decide citation quality. 600dpi with bundled OCR outputs searchable PDF and Word near 95 percent accuracy on clean prints, good enough for Find and quote checks. Check duplex support for double-sided handouts, card-to-legal width for mixed archives, and cloud export to Zotero, Drive or OneDrive. Weak drivers waste more time than slow hardware, so favor Epson, Brother and Fujitsu apps.

Power and portability close the call. USB-powered 11-ounce wands suit carrels with laptops and skip bricks, while battery plus Wi-Fi models scan phone-direct without outlets for true cordless aisles. Single-sheet feeds are lightest but slowest, multi-page hoppers cost more, and refurbished flagships stretch budgets near 100 dollars. Always confirm return policies for rare-book work. Export OCR straight to Zotero or Drive weekly so scans stay citable, not just images.
