    Failed Dissertation in the UK? Resit and Resubmission Options

    What happens after a failed UK dissertation: resit, resubmission, mark caps, mitigating circumstances and appeals, with a practical revision plan.

    failed dissertationdissertation resubmissionresit optionsmitigating circumstancesUK universities

    Quick Answer

    What can you do after failing a dissertation in the UK?

    You can usually be reassessed once, by resit or resubmission, but the mark cap, deadline, fee and eligibility threshold are set by your university. Get your outcome and examiner feedback in writing, check whether a mitigating circumstances claim is possible before its deadline, and rework the failed dissertation on a fixed schedule of supervisor drafts. An appeal normally needs a procedural problem, not only disagreement with the mark.

    What Happens After a Failed Dissertation

    You opened the result and the number is below the pass mark. Before anything else, know that this is a recoverable situation at most UK universities. Students in public forums describe the same sequence: shock, shame, a day of not telling anyone, then a scramble to learn what "fail" actually means for their degree. This guide covers the second part. It explains what happens next, what changes between universities, and how to turn a failed dissertation into a passed resubmission.

    One caveat first. There is no single UK rule. Mark caps, deadlines, fees and the number of attempts are set by each university, and often differ between undergraduate, taught postgraduate and research degrees. The examples below come from published regulations. Use them to know what to ask, then check your own handbook.

    First, Find Out Exactly What "Failed" Means for You

    Three details decide almost everything that follows, so get them in writing from your programme office or exam board before you plan anything:

    • Your mark band. Several universities set a floor for resubmission. A University of Manchester handbook allows resubmission at 30 or above and says students below 30 will not be permitted to resubmit. Warwick's Philosophy MA page says a mark below 30 will normally allow a reworked submission only for a Postgraduate Diploma.
    • Whether the new mark is capped. A cap means a resubmission can earn no more than the pass mark, however good it is.
    • Whether this counts as a first or a second attempt. If accepted mitigating circumstances turn the failed sitting into a "null sit", the next submission is treated as a first attempt, which usually removes the cap.

    Official exam board decisions are also worded differently from what students expect. "Refer", "fail with resit", "fail with retake" and "fail with resubmission" are not interchangeable. If your outcome letter uses a term you don't recognise, ask what it means in practice.

    What Published Regulations Say: Five Examples

    UCL (taught programmes)Dissertations normally get a resit rather than a repeat. A passed resit is capped at the pass mark. A failed second attempt can mean leaving the programme. Reassessment is expected within the next two academic sessions.Academic Manual, Chapter 4, Section 6

    Look at how different these are. Edinburgh gives three months and a cap of 50, Surrey gives six months, and UWE states that resits are not capped. A blanket claim that resubmissions are always capped is wrong often enough that you should never rely on it. Check the handbook for your year of entry, because regulations are revised.

    Resit, Retake and Repeat Are Different Things

    A resit usually means reassessment of the failed element only, with no additional teaching and often no fee. A retake or repeat means redoing the module with teaching, and it is usually charged. UCL, for example, says failure of up to 60 taught credits in a session is reassessed by resit, while larger failures generally require a repeat with tuition and fees. For most dissertations the realistic outcome is a resubmission of the same project, usually supervised, often within weeks or months.

    Mitigating Circumstances: Act Early, and Respect the Deadline

    If illness, bereavement, caring duties or a disability affected your work, the route is a mitigating or extenuating circumstances claim. The difference it can make is large: at Edinburgh and Imperial, a granted claim can mean the next attempt is uncapped.

    The practical problem is timing. In a public forum thread from 2016, a poster described a relative's claim being rejected because it was not submitted on the day results came out. That is one anecdote about one institution and it is old, so do not assume your university works the same way. It does show why you should look up your own deadline today, not after you have recovered from the shock. Gather evidence such as a doctor's note, a disability service letter or supervisor emails, and submit through the official process, not only by telling your tutor.

    ⚠

    Check your deadline today. Mitigating circumstances claims and appeals have their own time limits, which can be short. Look up yours in your university's assessment regulations before you plan anything else.

    Appeals: What Is Realistic

    An appeal and a resubmission are not alternatives you must choose between, but they need different amounts of effort. Commenters in a 2023 Mumsnet thread made the point that disagreeing with a mark is generally treated as academic judgement and is hard to appeal. Grounds that are accepted tend to be procedural, for example where the university did not follow its own regulations.

    The Office of the Independent Adjudicator (OIA) framework for England and Wales defines a procedural irregularity as a situation where a provider's procedures and regulations have not been complied with. The OIA expects you to complete your university's internal process first, and a complaint form must reach the OIA within 12 months of the Completion of Procedures Letter.

    The sensible approach is to run both tracks. Request feedback and plan the resubmission, and if you believe there was a process failure (a supervisor who left, missed feedback, a rejected claim that ignored policy), keep records and ask the students' union advice service about your appeal deadline. Do not let an appeal replace resubmission planning, because internal appeals can be lengthy (a point ResearchProspect, a commercial UK guide, also makes).

    Why Dissertations Fail, in Students' Own Words

    Public threads are not research data, but they show patterns that regulations never mention. These are summaries, not quotations, and none of the accounts has been independently verified.

    • Drafts were never sent. In a 2024 Reddit thread, a master's student who failed said they never sent drafts to their supervisor. Replies treated this as a common, fixable mistake and suggested asking the marker why it failed and agreeing regular draft deadlines.
    • Feedback was too vague to use. In a 2023 Mumsnet thread, a master's student with earlier marks in the 70s said the feedback did not show what to keep and what to rewrite. The marking grid cited insufficient and out-of-date literature and writing that was "too reflective". Some replies backed the student's frustration with the supervisor; others said students are expected to find current literature themselves and warned against blaming the supervisor.
    • Reused work created doubt. The same poster had extended a previously marked project and was unsure whether that counted as academic misconduct. Their supervisor did not answer. Reuse of your own earlier work is something to ask about before submission, not after.
    • Supervision broke down. In the 2016 thread mentioned above, a mentor left partway through and a replacement was unavailable. Commenters advised escalating to the course director and keeping a record of meetings and calls.

    The disagreement is useful. Some readers will have a genuine supervision problem, and some will have a gap in their own planning. Most will have a bit of both. Whichever applies, the next step is the same: get the marker's reasons in writing.

    Questions to Ask When You Request Feedback

    1. Which assessment criteria did the dissertation fail to meet, and by how much?
    2. Which sections are acceptable and should be kept, and which need substantial rewriting?
    3. Is a new research question or new data allowed, or must I keep the same title and rework the same study? (Warwick's Philosophy page says to rework under the same title.)
    4. Will I keep my supervisor, and how many meetings or draft reviews are allowed?
    5. What is the resubmission deadline, and is the mark capped?

    Edinburgh's guidance for the capped route describes minor revisions that "should not require additional research" and a statement explaining why the first submission was deficient. If your university offers a similar statement, ask for it.

    A Resubmission Plan That Works

    Surrey and Warwick both point students toward reworking the failed dissertation, not starting from nothing. A workable structure for a three to six month window:

    • Weeks 1–2: diagnose. Compare the marking grid with your text, section by section. Turn each criticism into a task.
    • Weeks 3–6: repair the foundations. Update the literature review, tighten the research question, fix the methods justification and any analysis errors.
    • Weeks 7–10: rewrite results and discussion. Link each finding to your question and the literature. Keep a limitations section that is specific, not generic.
    • Final weeks: check and submit. Check referencing, word limit, formatting, similarity report, and any required declaration about AI use. Submit before the deadline.

    Send drafts to your supervisor on a fixed schedule. Agreeing regular draft deadlines was one of the practical fixes commenters suggested in the 2024 Reddit thread.

    Before you resubmit, a consultation on your feedback, structure and methods can help. You can request Dissertation support that focuses on what your examiners actually asked you to fix.

    Academic Integrity Can End a Resubmission

    If your fail followed a plagiarism or misconduct finding, the rules are different. A commercial UK dissertation guide (ResearchProspect) states that plagiarism can prevent resubmission and that appeals against plagiarism findings are generally not possible. That is a secondary source, so confirm it in your university's misconduct regulations. Whatever the rule, never submit work you did not write, and declare AI assistance exactly as your university's policy requires.

    The Money and the Feelings

    Students describe two worries beyond the mark: the money already spent and what the failure says about them. Some replies in the 2024 thread told the poster that the grade does not define them and that a failed essay is easier to repair than a failed exam. Others were blunter and told the student to take responsibility. Both are fair, and neither replaces a plan.

    Resits are often free (UWE, Edinburgh) while retakes are charged, so ask about fees before assuming the cost. If you are struggling, tell your personal tutor or the university wellbeing service. A failed dissertation also does not have to be hidden from the people who can help you.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can I resubmit a failed dissertation in the UK?
    Usually yes, once, subject to your mark band and your university's rules. Some universities refuse resubmission below a threshold mark, so check yours.
    Will my resubmitted dissertation be capped?
    Often at the pass mark, but not always. UWE states resits are not capped, and Edinburgh and Imperial both describe uncapped attempts after accepted exceptional or mitigating circumstances.
    Can I change my topic?
    It depends. Surrey and Warwick describe reworking the original, and a different title may need approval. Ask the exam board in writing.
    What if I fail the resubmission?
    The outcome can range from a lower award, such as a postgraduate diploma, to leaving the programme. UCL says a student who fails a second attempt must leave the programme, though other awards may be possible. Check the exit awards in your regulations.
    How long do I have to appeal?
    Internal deadlines are set by your university and can be short. In one 2016 forum thread, a commenter mentioned 20 working days at their institution. The OIA's own limit is 12 months from your Completion of Procedures Letter.
