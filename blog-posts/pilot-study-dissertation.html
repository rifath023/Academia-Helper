    What Is a Pilot Study and Do You Need One for Your Dissertation?

    A practical guide to using a small pilot to find problems in questions, timing, procedures and data before the main study begins.

    pilot study dissertationquestionnaire pilot testresearch pilotpretest surveydata collection

    Quick Answer

    Do you need to pilot a dissertation questionnaire?

    In many survey, interview and experimental student projects, a pilot is useful because it can reveal unclear wording, timing problems, technical errors and weak procedures before the main study. Whether it is required depends on your methodology, ethics approval, instrument and university guidance.

    What Is a Pilot Study?

    A pilot study is a small-scale test of important parts of the planned research process. It may test the questionnaire, interview guide, recruitment procedure, experiment, data-entry workflow or analysis plan before the main data collection begins.

    A pilot has a learning purpose: identify problems, revise the procedure and reduce avoidable errors in the main study.

    Pilot Study vs Questionnaire Pretest

    A questionnaire pretest focuses mainly on whether questions are understandable, response options work and the survey flows logically. A broader pilot may also test recruitment, consent, timing, data export and analysis procedures.

    Universities use the terms differently, so define what you actually did rather than relying only on the label.

    What Should You Test?

    Test wording, response options, completion time, instructions, consent, recording or survey technology, recruitment steps and data export. If the study uses a questionnaire, check whether variables arrive in the format needed for analysis.

    Do not ask only whether participants liked the survey. Ask which item was difficult, which option did not fit and what instruction was unclear.

    Who Should Join the Pilot?

    Pilot participants should resemble the target population closely enough to reveal realistic problems. If the final survey is for postgraduate international students, testing only with staff may miss language and interpretation issues.

    Decide in advance whether pilot data could be included later. Major changes to the instrument usually make simple merging less defensible.

    Use Pilot Results to Improve the Study

    Simplify confusing language, fix incomplete response options, correct technical logic and remove redundant items. Keep a record of the original issue and the change made.

    If the study already has ethics approval, substantial changes to participant materials or procedures may require an amendment. Check institutional rules before continuing.

    How to Report a Pilot Study

    State who participated, what was tested, what issues were identified and which changes were made. You do not need to list every spelling correction, but meaningful methodological changes should be transparent.

    If no major changes were required, say so. The value of a pilot is that the procedure was tested, not that it must produce dramatic problems.

    Common Pilot-Study Mistakes

    Calling the first main-study responses a pilot after problems appear, testing only with people unlike the target population, asking only whether the survey looked fine, and failing to document changes are common mistakes.

    Pilot the data workflow as well as the participant experience. Export the pilot responses and test variable names, coding, missing values, scale scoring and software import. A survey can look perfect on screen but still create an unusable data file. Fixing that problem before full collection can save substantial time.

    Practical Final Check

    This step can reveal problems that participants will not report. A questionnaire may feel easy to complete but export age as text, split one response across several columns or produce confusing variable labels. Solving these issues before full data collection can save many hours.

    After collecting pilot responses, export the data and attempt the first stages of cleaning and analysis. Check whether variable names are understandable, response categories are coded correctly, missing values appear as expected and reverse-scored items can be identified. For interviews, check recording quality and whether the transcript format is usable.

    Pilot the Data Workflow, Not Just the Questions

    This prevents arbitrary changes based on one participant's personal preference. A pilot is most useful when feedback is interpreted systematically and revisions are linked to evidence rather than made simply because someone suggested different wording.

    Before the pilot, identify the kinds of problems that would justify changing the instrument. Examples include repeated misunderstanding, high item non-response, impossible response options, very long completion time or failed skip logic.

    Decide What Would Trigger a Revision

    Treat the pilot as a decision point rather than a box to tick. After reviewing feedback and pilot data, write a short record explaining what will remain unchanged, what will be revised and why. This note can later support the methodology chapter and demonstrate that the main study was not launched blindly. If the pilot shows that a key procedure is unreliable or confusing, it is better to redesign before recruitment than to protect a weak plan simply because work has already started.

    Key Takeaways

    • Use a pilot to test the research process.
    • Recruit pilot participants similar to the target population.
    • Check wording, timing, technology and analysis readiness.
    • Ask specific feedback questions.
    • Document changes after the pilot.
    • Follow ethics requirements for pilot recruitment and amendments.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How many people should be in a pilot?
    There is no universal number. The pilot should be large enough to reveal practical issues relevant to the design.
    Can pilot participants be in the final sample?
    Sometimes, especially if nothing material changes, but the decision must be methodologically and ethically defensible.
    Do qualitative interviews need a pilot?
    A practice or pilot interview can be very useful for testing the guide, timing, recording and probes.
    Is a pilot the same as a feasibility study?
    They overlap, but feasibility work often focuses more broadly on whether the study can be conducted.
    What if the pilot finds no problems?
    Report that the procedure was tested and no major changes were necessary.
    Does a pilot need ethics approval?
    Often yes when people provide research data or test participant-facing procedures. Follow institutional rules.
