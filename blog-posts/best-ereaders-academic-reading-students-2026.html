How We Chose: Buying Guide

Screen size is the first filter and it depends on your files. Reflowed ebooks, Kindle novels and EPUB library loans read beautifully on six-inch 300ppi screens that slip into a jacket pocket and last weeks per charge. Scanned image PDFs, two-column journal layouts and A4 textbook pages need zooming and panning on six inches, so students in PDF-heavy modules should favor seven inches or a 10-inch pen model. If most readings arrive as scanned chapters, budget for the larger screen rather than fighting pinch-zoom all term.

Library access splits Kindle and Kobo. Kobo readers handle EPUB natively with OverDrive built in, so borrowing from university and public libraries is direct. Kindles borrow through Libby Send-to-Kindle or store purchases and Kindle Unlimited, with a larger English ebook store but an extra conversion step for EPUB files. Check which system your university library supports before committing, because the wrong ecosystem adds weekly friction.

Comfort features decide long-session winners. Adjustable warm lighting that shifts amber in the evening reduces harshness during late essay reading, waterproofing protects against coffee and fieldwork rain, and physical page-turn buttons speed seminar skimming versus touch swipes. For annotation, only pen models like the Scribe let you write directly on PDFs, while standard readers export highlights to apps for Zotero and Notion. Storage of 16GB holds thousands of books, so prioritize screen, light and format support over gigabytes.

Finally, weigh budget honestly against the syllabus. The Basic Kindle undercuts everything and covers novels plus Send-to-Kindle PDFs, the Clara and Paperwhite add warm light and waterproofing for daily readers, and the Libra and Scribe charge premiums for buttons, size and pen input. Consider certified refurbished premium models to reach a bigger screen, and keep a cheap tablet or laptop alongside any six-inch reader for the occasional full-page scanned diagram. Pair any reader with a free reference manager and a weekly Send-to-Kindle routine, converting each module's readings every Sunday so the device is always ready for Monday seminars.
