Analyse vs Evaluate vs Describe: Command Words Examples That Get Distinctions

First-year and international students lose the most marks not on knowledge, but on one verb. Learn the analyse vs evaluate vs describe difference with UK definitions, a comparison table, and three full paragraphs on the SAME topic.

analyse vs evaluate vs describe difference evaluate vs describe analyse discuss essay command words examples study skills UK

You can know the topic perfectly and still get 48%. In UK universities, that almost always comes down to one thing: you answered the wrong command word. You described when the question asked you to analyse, or you analysed when it asked you to evaluate.

For first-year and international students, this is especially confusing. In many school systems, “evaluate” just means “write about” and “analyse” means “explain in detail.” In the UK, each verb triggers a completely different structure, different evidence, and a different grade band. If you want a full glossary, start with our guide to academic command words — then come back here to master the three verbs that decide Pass vs Distinction.

Quick answer — analyse vs evaluate vs describe difference: Describe = say what it is (summarise features, no judgement). Analyse = break it into parts and explain how and why the parts connect (causes, mechanisms, relationships). Evaluate = judge how strong, effective or convincing it is, using explicit criteria, weighed evidence, and a reasoned verdict. Description shows knowledge. Analysis shows understanding. Evaluation shows judgement — and judgement gets distinctions.

Why One Verb Changes Your Whole Grade

UK markers do not mark “effort” or “length.” They mark against assessment criteria linked directly to the command word. A typical Level 4–6 rubric looks like this: 40–49% (Pass) = accurate description; 50–59% = description plus limited explanation; 60–69% (Merit) = clear analysis with connections and supporting evidence; 70%+ (Distinction) = sustained evaluation with criteria, counter-arguments, and weighing.

That is why two students can write 1,500 words on remote working and get 20 marks apart. Student A lists what remote working is, how many people do it, and what companies offer it. Student B explains why remote working raises productivity for some roles but lowers it for others, then judges under what conditions it is most effective. Same topic, same reading list — different verb, different grade.

International students face an extra trap: translation. In some languages, “analyse” and “evaluate” translate to near-synonyms. In UK academic English they sit at different levels of Bloom’s taxonomy. Describe is remember + understand. Analyse is analyse — differentiate, organise, deconstruct. Evaluate is evaluate — check, critique, judge. You cannot substitute one for another without dropping a grade boundary.

If your brief says evaluate an assignment question, assume you must still describe briefly (15–20% of words) and analyse (40%) before you evaluate (40%). Evaluation without prior analysis reads as unsupported opinion. For a deeper look at discussion verbs, see critically discuss meaning.

1. Describe = Summarise What It Is (No Judgement)

In UK glossaries (Oxford, Cambridge, Leeds, Manchester study-skills pages), describe consistently means: give a detailed account of the main characteristics, what something is like, or what happened. You do not explain why. You do not judge whether it is good. You show the marker you know the facts.

When to describe

  • The question says describe, outline, summarise, state, what are the main features of…
  • The opening 2–3 sentences of an analyse or evaluate answer (scene-setting only)
  • Methods, definitions, timelines, models — before you use them

Sentence starters that keep you in describe mode

  • Remote working is defined as… It typically involves…
  • The main features include… For example…
  • According to ONS (2024), approximately…
✍️ Example — Describe paragraph (Pass level, ~190 words)

Question: Describe the impact of remote working on employee productivity.

Remote working, also called homeworking or teleworking, is a work arrangement in which employees perform their duties outside a central office, typically from home, using digital tools such as Zoom, Teams and Slack. According to the UK Office for National Statistics (2024), around 40% of UK employees worked remotely at least part of the week, compared with 12% in 2019. The main features of remote working include flexible hours, reduced commuting, and reliance on cloud-based collaboration. In terms of productivity, several surveys report mixed patterns. A CIPD (2023) survey found that 41% of employers believed productivity had increased, while 18% reported a decrease. Commonly reported positive effects include fewer office distractions and longer uninterrupted work periods. Commonly reported negative effects include communication delays, feelings of isolation, and difficulty separating work and home life. Overall, remote working involves a shift from office-based supervision to output-based management, supported by performance dashboards and regular virtual check-ins.

Marker note (42%): Accurate, well-referenced summary. Covers what, how many, and what effects are reported. But there is no why — no mechanism linking flexibility to output — and no judgement. Perfect for a describe question; capped at Pass if submitted for analyse or evaluate.

2. Analyse = Break Down + Explain Relationships

Analyse means to separate a whole into its components and examine how those components relate: causes and effects, similarities and differences, mechanisms and conditions. UK study guides stress the same formula: identify parts → explain relationships → show significance. If describe answers what, analyse answers why and how.

Analysis needs at least two ideas in tension. Remote working does not “increase” or “decrease” productivity in the abstract — it increases individual task productivity through fewer interruptions, while decreasing collaborative productivity through weaker informal communication. That distinction is the analysis.

Sentence starters that force analysis

  • This occurs because… The underlying mechanism is…
  • Whereas X leads to…, Y leads to… This suggests…
  • A key distinction is between… This relationship is moderated by…
✍️ Example — Analyse paragraph (Merit level, ~230 words)

Question: Analyse the impact of remote working on employee productivity.

Remote working affects productivity through two opposing mechanisms, which explains why survey results appear mixed. First, it increases individual task productivity because it removes office-based interruptions and commuting fatigue. When employees control their schedule, they can align demanding tasks with peak concentration periods, which therefore extends periods of deep work (Bloom et al., 2023). This mechanism is strongest for roles with measurable individual outputs, such as software development and data entry, where output depends more on sustained focus than on real-time coordination. Second, however, remote working weakens collaborative productivity because it reduces spontaneous, high-bandwidth communication. Whereas co-located teams resolve ambiguity through quick informal exchanges, remote teams rely on scheduled calls and written updates, which therefore slows feedback loops and increases misunderstanding (Golden et al., 2022). This negative effect is moderated by task interdependence: the more a role depends on joint problem-solving, as in product design or client consulting, the greater the productivity cost. Consequently, the overall impact of remote working is not uniform but conditional — it enhances productivity where autonomy is high and interdependence is low, and suppresses it where the reverse holds.

Marker note (65%): Clear breaking-down (two mechanisms), causal language (because, therefore, whereas, moderated by), and a linking conclusion (conditional effect). Sources are used to explain, not just to state. To reach 70%+, this needs explicit criteria and a weighed verdict — i.e., evaluation.

3. Evaluate = Judge With Evidence and Criteria

Evaluate means to make a judgement about value, effectiveness, strength or validity — and to support that judgement with weighed evidence against explicit criteria. Every UK university repeats the same three requirements: criteria + evidence + verdict. Without stated criteria, evaluation collapses into opinion. Without counter-evidence, it collapses into one-sided argument.

Think of evaluation as a courtroom. The claim (“remote working improves productivity”) is on trial. You present the best evidence for it, the best evidence against it, you state the legal test (your criteria: e.g., output quality, sustainability, generalisability), then you deliver a reasoned verdict. Hedged, precise, and comparative — never “I personally feel.”

Sentence starters that signal evaluation

  • The most convincing evidence for… is… However, its strength is limited by…
  • Judged against the criteria of… X is more effective than… because…
  • On balance / Overall, despite…, the stronger argument is…
✍️ Example — Evaluate paragraph (Distinction level, ~260 words)

Question: Evaluate the impact of remote working on employee productivity.

Judged against the criteria of output quality, sustainability, and generalisability, the claim that remote working improves productivity is only partially convincing. The strongest evidence in its favour comes from Bloom et al.’s (2023) randomised trial of 1,600 call-centre workers, which found a 13% rise in output, driven primarily by fewer breaks and quieter conditions. This study is persuasive because of its experimental design and large sample, and it is corroborated by ONS (2024) data showing self-reported gains among workers in autonomous roles. However, this evidence is significantly weakened on grounds of generalisability and sustainability. Golden et al. (2022) demonstrate that in highly interdependent roles, remote working reduced team output by 8–11% due to slower feedback and weaker knowledge sharing — a limitation Bloom’s narrow, individual-task sample cannot address. Moreover, longitudinal data (CIPD, 2023) suggest early productivity gains fade after 12–18 months as isolation and blurred work–life boundaries increase burnout, undermining the sustainability criterion. While proponents argue that hybrid models resolve this trade-off, the evidence for hybrid superiority remains largely correlational and confounded by self-selection. On balance, therefore, remote working is most defensibly judged as effective for low-interdependence, individually measured work in the short to medium term, but ineffective as a universal productivity strategy. Its overall value depends on role design rather than location itself.

Marker note (74%): Explicit criteria up front, strongest evidence first, counter-evidence weighed (not just listed), methodological critique (generalisability, correlational limits), hedged verdict (partially, on balance, most defensibly). This is what distinction evaluation looks like in one paragraph.

Side-by-Side: Same Topic, Three Grades

Notice how the topic knowledge is identical across all three paragraphs — remote working, ONS data, Bloom and Golden studies. What changes is the thinking move:

Move Describe paragraph Analyse paragraph Evaluate paragraph
Opening Defines + gives statistics States two opposing mechanisms States criteria + initial verdict
Middle Lists positives and negatives Explains why each mechanism works + condition Presents best evidence, then best counter-evidence
Closing Restates shift to output management Concludes effect is conditional Delivers hedged judgement + scope limit
Grade logic Accurate = Pass Explained + connected = Merit Judged + weighed = Distinction

Practical tip for evaluate vs describe and analyse discuss essay questions: use the 20-40-40 rule. Spend roughly 20% of words describing (so the reader can follow), 40% analysing (mechanisms, distinctions, causes), and 40% evaluating (criteria, weighing, verdict). Students who spend 80% describing and add “In conclusion, it is good” in the last line are not evaluating — they are describing with an opinion tacked on.

5 Distinction Moves That Turn Analysis into Evaluation

1. State your criteria in the first two sentences

Markers cannot reward judgement if they do not know what you are judging by. Name 2–3 criteria: effectiveness, validity, reliability, applicability, sustainability, ethicality, cost-effectiveness. Example: “This policy will be judged on effectiveness, equity, and feasibility.” Every later paragraph should return to those words.

2. Steelman the counter-argument before you dismiss it

Distinction answers present the strongest version of the opposing view, then show why it still loses. Weak move: “Some say remote work is bad, but they are wrong.” Strong move: “The strongest objection is Golden et al.’s finding of an 11% team-output drop; however, this applies mainly to high-interdependence roles and therefore does not overturn the overall judgement for autonomous work.”

3. Weigh evidence, don’t stack it

Three weak sources do not beat one strong randomised trial. Comment on method: sample size, design (experimental vs correlational), date, context (UK vs US, call centre vs consultancy). Phrases like “more persuasive because…”, “limited by…”, “carries less weight since…” are explicit weighing signals markers scan for.

4. Hedge like an academic, not like you’re unsure

Distinction hedging is precision, not vagueness. Use partially, largely, tends to, under conditions where, on balance, suggests rather than proves. Avoid very, really, extremely, in my opinion. Hedging shows you understand limits — which is itself an evaluative skill.

5. End with a scoped verdict + “so what”

Every evaluate paragraph and essay needs a verdict that answers: to what extent, for whom, under what conditions, and what follows? Example: “Overall, hybrid working is the most defensible model for knowledge work, provided teams retain two co-located days for interdependent tasks; managers should therefore redesign roles around interdependence rather than mandating universal office return.”

💡 Tutor Tip

If you only change one habit, change your topic sentences. Descriptive topic sentence: “Another effect of remote working is isolation.” Analytical topic sentence: “Isolation reduces productivity because it weakens informal feedback, particularly in interdependent roles.” Evaluative topic sentence: “The isolation objection is the strongest limit on remote-working gains, though its weight is greatest where collaboration is essential.” Markers decide your band largely from these first lines.

Distinction Checklist: Before You Submit

  • Command word underlined? Does every section serve describe, analyse, or evaluate as asked — not all three equally?
  • Description under 20%? Highlight purely descriptive sentences in yellow. If more than a fifth is yellow in an evaluate essay, cut or convert.
  • Analysis signals present? Search for because, therefore, whereas, leads to, mechanism, moderated by. Fewer than 2 per paragraph = still descriptive.
  • Criteria explicit? Can a stranger name your 2–3 judgement criteria after reading your intro?
  • Counter + weighing? Is there a however / albeit / despite paragraph where you steelman then outweigh the objection?
  • Hedged verdict? Does your conclusion say to what extent, for whom, under what conditions — not just “it is good/bad”?
  • Referencing supports thinking? Are citations used to explain or judge, not just to state facts? (Harvard, APA as required.)

Run this checklist on one paragraph first using the examples above as models. Once a single describe → analyse → evaluate conversion feels natural, apply it across the whole essay. That one skill reliably lifts students from high Pass to Merit and Merit to Distinction.

You normally need to analyse first (break down causes and relationships) before you can evaluate fairly (judge which explanation or solution is strongest). An evaluate answer that does not analyse first usually drops to a merit or pass.

Add because, therefore, however, and leads to. After every descriptive sentence, ask: why does this happen, what does it connect to, and what is the mechanism? For example, do not just say remote working increased 40%. Explain that it increased because commuting costs rose and cloud tools removed co-location needs, which therefore shifted managerial control from presence to output.

Final Thoughts: Write to the Verb, Not Just the Topic

The analyse vs evaluate vs describe difference is the cheapest grade boost in UK study: same reading, same effort, higher band — just by matching your thinking move to the verb. Describe to show you know. Analyse to show you understand why. Evaluate to show you can judge — with criteria, counter-arguments, and a hedged, scoped verdict.

Keep the comparison table and the three remote-working paragraphs as templates. Next time you see evaluate vs describe or an analyse discuss essay, draft one descriptive sentence, force yourself to add because and whereas, then add on balance with criteria. Do that consistently and distinctions stop feeling like luck.
